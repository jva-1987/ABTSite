CET differential pressure air volume flow cascade (CetPFlCas11)

Overview

The application function "CET room pressurization differential pressure air volume flow cascade 11" (CetPFlCas11) calculates flow setpoints for supply and extract terminals in a room to accomplish pressurization. The difference between supply and extract flows is dynamically adjusted to drive measured room pressure to its configured setpoint. The setpoints are selected to accomplish heating, cooling, and ventilation within constraints of the minimum and maximum flow rates for the terminals.

This AF is used to maintain pressurization despite changes in the mechanical system. The value of the room flow offset reflects those changes.

Main features:

  • Differential pressure air volume flow cascade loop
  • Airflow coordination for pressurized rooms, including:
    • Single tracking pair (one supply, one extract)
    • Lab room with fume hood (one supply, one extract, and fume hood)
    • Large lab with fume hoods (multiple supply, extract, and fume hoods)
  • Coordination of airflow demands for heating, cooling, and ventilation
  • Airflow monitoring including warning and alarm values (configurable)
  • Multiple segment support
    (A segment can include any of the following: supply terminal / extract terminal / supply + extract pair / independent exhaust terminal - fume hood).

The AF can be set up with a switch to indicate the door is open, the room pressure loop is suspended, and the flow offset setting for the room does not change. When the switch indicates the door has closed, room pressure control resumes after a configured delay.

  • Alarms
    • Air flow transfer alarm
    • Room pressure alarm
  • Warnings
    • Air flow transfer warning
    • Room pressure warning
  • Flow monitoring values
    • Total supply
    • Total extract
    • Air flow transfer
  • Other supply/ other exhaust

CAUTION

Airflow contaminant alarms and control

To effectively manage air contaminants, users often need the BACS to alarm when the airflow tracking system does not operate properly. In addition, users often need to confirm the relationship between airflow control and contaminant control.

  1. Installing contractors and service technicians should work with end users to ensure alarms and operating procedures are effective.
  2. A room pressure monitor may be warranted or required.

Function

Room pressure PID
The room pressure PID adjusts the transfer airflow to drive the measured pressure to the setpoint (SpDiffPR). The range of adjustment for transfer airflow is set by two configuration value objects: minimum transferred air volume flow (AirFlTrnMin) and maximum transferred air volume flow (AirFlTrnMax).

PRMod
Room pressurization mode (PRMod) can be set for neutral, positive, or negative room pressure. The airflow setpoints are calculated in one of three ways (modes) depending on the setting of PRMod.

  • Positive - the supply flow equals exhaust plus transfer.
    Transfer flow is outward. Room pressure is higher than surroundings.
  • Negative - the exhaust flow equals supply plus transfer.
    Transfer flow is inward. Room pressure is lower than surroundings.
  • Neutral - the supply flow equals exhaust, and the AF does not use the transfer flow (flow offset) value.
    Net transfer flow is almost zero. Direction of transfer flow is unreliable. Room pressure is the same as surroundings.

DiffPR
Room differential pressure (DiffPR) is measured relative to a reference space. The reference may be an adjacent space or a selected, central location. A positive value means the pressure in the room is higher than the pressure in the reference location. This sign convention can match or differ from the sign convention for AirFlTrnR.

SpDiffPR
Room differential pressure setpoint (SpDiffPR) has the same sign convention as the measured pressure.

SpAirFlTrn
Transferred air volume flow setpoint (SpAirFlTrn) is the setpoint for the amount of air that enters or exits a pressurized room through openings in the room boundaries.

In a pressurized room, the extract airflow setpoint differs from the supply airflow setpoint by the value of SpAirFlTrn.

Example: In a negative pressure room, if supply = 600 and AirFlTrn = 100, the extract airflow setpoint will = 700. In a positive pressure room, if supply = 600 and AirFlTrn = 100, the extract airflow setpoint will = 500. (net flow direction is indicated by PRMod value)

AirFlTrnR
Room transferred air volume flow (AirFlTrnR) is the calculated value from all airflow measurements. It is calculated for monitoring and alarming. In this AF, the controller works to drive AirFlTrnR to the setpoint (SpAirFlTrn). (Setpoint signals sent to terminals in the segment(s) are scaled as a percentage.)

Operators monitoring pressurized rooms can check the value of AirFlTrnR. If it is a positive value, then the room is pressurized in the right direction; if negative, then air flow transfer is opposite of the intended direction.

  • When PRMod is set to Positive (or Neutral), transfer flow is defined as supply minus extract
  • When PRMod is set to Negative, transfer flow is defined as extract minus supply

AirFlSuR
Room supply air volume flow(AirFlSuR) is the total supply room air volume flow. This includes supply terminals in the segments and AirFlSuAdd.

AirFlExR
Room extract air volume flow (AirFlExR) is the total extract room air volume flow. This includes exhaust terminals in the segments and AirFlExAdd.

AirFlTrnMax
Maximum transferred air volume flow (AirFlTrnMax) is the high limiting value for automatically calculated AirFlTrnR. Same sign convention as AirFlTrnR.

AirFlTrnMin
Minimum transferred air volume flow(AirFlTrnMin) is the low limiting value for automatically calculated AirFlTrnR. Same sign convention as AirFlTrnR.

Fume hood flow
Collected from the fume hoods that are configured as members of the CetRCtl group. The AF handles changes in the fume hood flow by adjusting supply, extract, or both.

Other supply / other extract
The AF collects dynamic flow data from fume hoods and recalculates the supply and extract setpoints. This AF handles other supply and extract devices that are outside its control but are part of the room air balance. The other flow data may be static (configured) or dynamic (written by another device on the network).

Troubleshooting Pressurization and Airflow Setpoint Tracking

Normally, differential room pressure (DiffPR) should remain closely aligned with both its setpoint (SpDiffPR) and other pressurization requirements (PRMod, etc). In rare cases, persistent errors in pressurization and airflow may appear, including:

  1. An inability of one or more air terminals associated with the room to meet its airflow setpoint
  2. Transfer flow (AirFlTrnR) off of setpoint (SpAirFlTrn) or cycling around it, potentially causing a switchover in the room from positive to negative pressure, or vice-versa
  3. Differential pressure consistently above or below the setpoint.

These problems may be caused by PID logic that does not effectively coordinate the airflow of each terminal. In this situation, the system will “hang” in a mismatched state, where the airflow setpoint for each terminal is not driven to meet the total transfer airflow required for the room pressure setpoint (SpDiffPR). Solving the problem will require you to adjust the approach taken by the PID controller. Instead of letting the trailing terminals track the calculated setpoint of the leading terminal, the tracking method must use the actual airflow in the leading terminal as the setpoint.

There are at least two options to achieve this result, depending on how aggressive the user chooses to be about switching from setpoint tracking (the default) to airflow tracking. The user should keep in mind that there are trade-offs when using airflow tracking. Under this mode, the calculated setpoint of trailing terminals will fluctuate based on the current airflow in the leading terminal. Constant changes in setpoint make the overall performance of the system more variable and harder to tune. Some time will elapse as the trailing terminal(s) catch up to the new setpoint, which may cause brief errors in overall airflow and room pressure. Broadcast network traffic for this segment may increase. For these reasons, the system uses setpoint tracking when possible.

Directly Switch the Tracking Method (TckMthd) from Setpoint to Air Volume Flow

If the tracking method (TckMthd) for differential pressure is set to “Air Volume Flow,” the tracking air terminal(s) will immediately adjust their airflow setpoint based only on the actual (measured) airflow in the leading terminal. Use caution when enabling airflow tracking on an indefinite basis . See the next option for a more selective approach to using air flow tracking.

Set a Tolerance for Switching to Air Flow Tracking

It may be possible to keep setpoint tracking in effect some of the time. Both individual terminals and the PID can be configured so that they switch away from setpoint tracking only when the current deviation from the setpoint moves outside of an acceptable tolerance. There are at least two distinct methods for switching the tracking method:

  1. Each terminal uses its own tolerance parameter, SwiTolTckMthd, that determines when it switches from setpoint to airflow tracking. For the details of this functionality, see any of the relevant BAF pages for VAV supply and extract (for example, CetVavEx12, CetVavSu12).
  2. The flow tracking system for the room uses an independent tolerance logic for switching. This switch occurs when there is a deviation from the transfer flow setpoint. It switches both (1) airflow tracking in the trailing terminal and (2) the use of transfer flow for pressure setpoint calculations run by the PID. This implementation is outlined in the next section, Flow Tracking Switchover Logic.

Flow Tracking Switchover Logic

If the transfer flow moves away from the setpoint by an allowable amount, two areas switch away from setpoint tracking: (1) the trailing terminals switch to tracking airflow, and (2) the PID switches one of the values used by its pressure setpoint calculation: from using the transfer flow setpoint to using the current transfer flow.

To determine when to switch, the flow tracking system compares the following three configuration values and selects the smallest as the switchover criterion:

  • The setting for transfer flow warning tolerance (TolAflTrnWarn), if the warning is enabled
  • The setting for transfer flow alarm tolerance (TolAirFlTrnAlm), if the alarm is enabled
  • 25% of the transfer flow setpoint (SpAirFlTrn)

When the deviation from the transfer flow setpoint exceeds the smallest of these criteria, both the trailing terminals and the PID switch at the same time.

For diagnostic purposes, it may be helpful to understand the logic of the switchover performed by the PID. The PID is responsible for maintaining the correct level of differential pressure. To achieve the pressure setpoint, the PID takes into account multiple values in the room. Among the inputs to the PID is the value for transfer flow. Under normal conditions, the input to the PID’s pressure calculation is the transfer flow setpoint. But when the value for the current transfer flow exceeds the configured tolerance for deviation from the transfer flow setpoint, the PID will switch to using the current transfer flow. At this point, current transfer flow will be used by the PID logic that maintains the pressure setpoint. When the PID uses current transfer flow instead of setpoint, this has the effect of preventing a high and persistent level of deviation from setpoint.

Guidelines for Tuning Pressure Control

When configuring values for the transfer flow warning (TolAflTrnWarn), and alarm (TolAirFlTrnAlm), try to set the smallest tolerance level that results in acceptable room pressure.

Set the tolerance small enough to cause the signal selector to use the airflow value collected from the terminals in each segment. Account for the overall size of flow error at the leading terminal.

When tuning individual terminals, make sure the dampers in each segment are sending measured flow values if they are not close enough to the setpoint. This requires you to check the tracking value object in the segment and confirm that is not sending back the value for the setpoint. If it is sending setpoint, adjust the switch tolerance, SwiTolTckMthd, and switch delay settings so the segment sends the current airflow value when it is too far from the setpoint.

Setting a low value for the transfer flow tolerance helps drive the transfer flow to the setpoint, but it can trigger tracking logic that keeps the room pressure PID from adjusting the transfer flow setpoint as needed.

Configure the PID to respond more actively to the current error in room pressure by increasing the controller gain. Gain is a configuration setting on the air flow controller. The default value for gain is set to respond slowly to conditions that affect room pressure over a long time. In this case, a stronger response is required to break the PID out of the tracking trap. Use the gain calculations below as a guide, adjusting them upward if the room pressure remains stable.

For many typical sizing scenarios:

Gain = 1000 %/inWC; Gain = 4 %/Pa

For uncommon sizing scenarios, experiment with the system to find the value of transfer flow that commonly corresponds to room pressure at the setpoint, QT0.
